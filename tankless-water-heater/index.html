Tankless water heater service in Coconut Creek, FL

A tankless water heater heats water on demand as it passes through a heat exchanger, so there is no storage tank to run out. In Coconut Creek, FL, we install gas and electric tankless units, repair them when something faults, and flush them to remove mineral scale.

Some callers just say the heater needs a service, or that it is time for a cleaning. Others have an error code on the display or lukewarm water. All of those lead to the same short list of causes, which we sort out below.

My tankless heater needs a service: what that usually means

People rarely say heat exchanger scale or flow sensor fault. They describe what they notice.

Hot water takes longer to arrive

Scale restricts flow through the heat exchanger or a screen is partly blocked.

Water temperature swings

A mineral-coated sensor, a dirty inlet filter or flow dropping below the minimum.

An error code on the display

Ignition, venting, flow or sensor faults. The code points us to the section to check.

Rumbling or kettling sounds

Scale boiling water in small pockets inside the exchanger.

It shuts off in mid-shower

Overheat or flow safety trips, often linked to scale or low gas or power supply.

It has never been flushed

The unit is a few years old with no maintenance record, so a flush is overdue.

Tankless flush and descale

A flush circulates a descaling solution through the heat exchanger with a small pump, then rinses it with clean water. It removes the mineral layer that builds up on the hot side of the exchanger.

We also clean the cold water inlet filter, check the gas or electrical connections, inspect the vent and test the unit across a range of flow rates. A flush takes the better part of an hour for most units, and we confirm exactly what we found.

How often depends on your water. Tap water that is softened and treated at the Broward County plant behaves differently from hard well water, but the city utility does not publish a hardness figure in its 2025 water quality report. Ask the utility for its latest numbers and then match the flush interval to the unit maker's guidance.

Gas or electric tankless

Fuel choice affects install cost and how the unit performs in a busy household.

Gas tankless

  • Higher flow rates for multiple fixtures.
  • Needs a gas line sized for the burner and a vent to outside.
  • Condensing models use plastic venting and a condensate drain.
  • Has ignition and combustion parts that need checking.

Electric tankless

  • No venting and a smaller footprint.
  • Needs a heavy dedicated circuit, often several breakers.
  • Lower flow rates, so best for fewer fixtures.
  • Panel capacity in older homes must be reviewed.

Installing a tankless heater

Tankless installation is a bigger job than a tank swap. The gas line may need upsizing, the vent runs to outside, and the wall needs room for the unit and clearances.

Many homeowners also add an isolation valve kit so flushing is quick in the future. We size the unit by flow rate and temperature rise, not by tank gallons. For a broader look at timing and options, see our water heater installation and water heater replacement pages.

Tankless in Coconut Creek: what matters locally

A few local points affect how we plan a tankless job.

Chloramine in the supply

The city utility disinfects with chloramines, according to its 2025 water quality report. Most tankless units handle this fine, but rubber seals and gaskets are worth checking at service time.

Scale control

Water at the county plant is treated to reduce hardness. The figure is not printed in the utility report, so ask the utility and set the flush schedule from the manufacturer guidance.

Storm season

Hurricane season runs June 1 to November 30. A gas tankless unit needs power for the controls, so outages mean no hot water until power returns. Electric units draw more load when the grid comes back.

Permits

The city Building Department notes that permits cover alterations and replacements, including plumbing. Call 954-973-6750 to confirm what your tankless project requires.

What affects the cost of tankless service

Prices vary with the job. These are the real cost factors.

Install or service

A flush costs much less than a new install.

Unit brand and size

Larger units and condensing models cost more to install and parts vary.

Fuel line and vent work

Upsizing gas lines or running new vents adds scope.

Isolation valves

A valve kit makes future flushes quicker and is itemized.

Condition of the unit

Heavy scale may need extra descaling time or part replacement.

Insurance generally treats tankless failure from age or scale as maintenance, not a covered loss. A sudden leak that floods a room may be covered, so keep your service records and photos handy.

Safety steps before you touch a tankless unit

A few checks keep a small problem from becoming a hazard, and none of them require opening the unit.

  • Turn off power at the breaker before you remove any cover, and never work on a gas unit yourself.
  • If you smell gas, leave the home and call the gas utility from outside. Do not switch lights or appliances.
  • Water at the outlet can be very hot, so test it with the back of your hand before use.
  • Do not run descaling solution through the unit unless the manual and the model allow it.
