How to Find a Hidden Water Leak in Coconut Creek, FL

Most hidden leaks can be confirmed with a water meter and ten minutes. This guide walks through the home tests, what the results mean and the point where a plumber with locating equipment saves you time.

Start with the water meter

A water meter counts every gallon that passes through the line, so it also catches the ones you did not mean to use. Find the meter in the box near the street or at the side of the house and lift the lid carefully, since insects and debris sometimes collect under it.

Turn off every fixture, the ice maker, the washing machine and the irrigation timer. Read the meter or look at the small low-flow indicator, a dial or triangle that spins at very slow flow. Wait fifteen minutes, then check again. Any movement means water is escaping somewhere.

The three-test sequence

Run these in order. Each narrows where the leak can be.

  1. 1

    Meter test

    With everything off, watch the indicator. If it moves, you have a leak somewhere between the meter and the fixtures.

  2. 2

    Main shutoff test

    Close the main valve at the house and check the meter again. If it stops, the leak is inside. If it keeps moving, it is in the line between the meter and the house.

  3. 3

    Fixture tests

    Check toilets, outdoor spigots, the water heater and irrigation lines one at a time to find which one is the source.

The toilet dye test

A toilet that leaks silently from the tank into the bowl can waste a lot of water and rarely makes noise. Take the lid off the tank, add a few drops of food coloring and wait fifteen minutes without flushing.

If color appears in the bowl, the flapper is not sealing. A worn flapper is one of the cheapest leaks to fix and one of the most common. See our toilet repair page if the problem is bigger than the flapper.

Quick checks you can do in minutes

These cost nothing and rule out the usual suspects.

  • Look under every sink for drips, damp wood or a musty smell.
  • Check the base of the water heater for rust, puddles or corrosion on the fittings.
  • Inspect washing machine hoses and the valves behind them.
  • Walk the yard and look for soft or unusually green ground near the line.
  • Check the ceiling in rooms below bathrooms for stains or bubbling paint.

Which utility reads your meter, and what the sprinkler rules say

The City of Coconut Creek utility serves residents north of Coconut Creek Parkway, and residents south of the Parkway are served by the City of Margate utility. If your meter test shows a leak, the meter itself belongs to the utility, so a damaged meter or a leaking connection at the meter box is their call.

The sprinkler schedule is also worth checking before you decide a lawn is leaking. Broward County restricts irrigation year-round, with no watering on Monday, Tuesday or Friday and only before 10 a.m. or after 4 p.m. on permitted days. A sprinkler running outside those windows may be a timer problem, and a wet lawn on a no-water day is a good reason to look at the irrigation valves.

Source: the City of Coconut Creek water services page and the Broward County irrigation restrictions page (municipal and county agency pages).

When to stop and call a plumber

If the main shutoff test shows the leak is in the buried line or under the slab, you cannot find it by looking. That is when listening equipment and thermal imaging save the most time and damage.

Our water line repair team handles leaks in the line from the meter to the house, and our leak detection technicians pinpoint leaks inside the walls and floors. Customers in Margate and Parkland call us for the same tests.

Do it yourself or call

Home tests are worth the time. They have limits.

You can handle

  • Reading the meter and running the shutoff test.
  • Dye testing toilets.
  • Checking visible fixtures and hoses.
  • Shutting off the main and photographing damage.

Call a pro for

  • A moving meter when every fixture checks out.
  • Warm spots, hissing or wet ground you cannot trace.
  • Water coming through a ceiling or wall.
  • Any leak near electrical equipment.
