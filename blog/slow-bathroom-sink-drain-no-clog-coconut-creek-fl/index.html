Why Is My Sink Draining Slowly in Coconut Creek, FL?

A sink that drains slowly is a warning, not yet a blockage. The cause is usually close to the fixture, in the stopper, the trap or a film that has built up on the pipe wall, and a few simple checks find it.

Is it the sink or the line?

The first question is where the slowdown starts. A problem at the fixture affects one sink only. A problem in the line affects every fixture that shares it. Run the other sinks, the tub and the washing machine and watch for gurgling, slow drainage or water that appears in the wrong place.

If only one sink is slow, the cause is probably in the stopper, the trap or the short pipe behind the wall. If several are slow, skip the home checks and call, because the blockage is further down.

Checks to try, from easiest to most involved

Work in this order so you do not take the pipes apart for nothing.

  1. 1

    Clean the stopper

    Remove the stopper or strainer and wipe away hair and soap. In many bathroom sinks the pop-up linkage under the sink lifts out for cleaning.

  2. 2

    Flush with hot tap water

    Run hot tap water for a minute to loosen light grease or soap film.

  3. 3

    Plunge

    Block the overflow opening with a wet rag, seal the plunger over the drain and plunge firmly.

  4. 4

    Clean the P-trap

    Place a bucket under the curved trap, loosen the slip nuts by hand and clear out the contents. Reassemble and run water to check for leaks.

  5. 5

    Call a plumber

    If water still drains slowly, the blockage is past the trap. A cable or jetting pass clears it.

What the P-trap does and why it clogs

The P-trap is the curved section of pipe under the sink. It holds a small plug of water that blocks sewer gas from rising into the room. Because the pipe curves, it is also where hair, soap and grease settle first.

Cleaning it is a small job. Put a bucket underneath, loosen the nuts and empty the trap. Look at what comes out, since the pattern tells you a lot. Hair and soap point to bathroom habits. A greasy sludge points to the kitchen.

Partial clogs and film that builds over time

Most slow drains are not blocked yet. Soap scum, grease and hair coat the inside of the pipe and narrow it, so water squeezes through a smaller opening. The sink still drains, only slowly, and it gets worse a little each week.

A chemical cleaner may open a path for a while, but it does not remove the film. A cable or a pass with high-pressure water scours the wall instead. Our hydro jetting page explains that method for lines that keep narrowing.

Venting: when the pipe cannot breathe

Drain pipes need air behind the water so it can flow. A vent pipe lets air in. If the vent is blocked, the water slows, the sink gurgles and the trap may lose its seal.

A vent problem is not a DIY job for most homeowners. If a sink drains slowly and gurgles, and the trap is clean, ask a plumber to check the vent. It may take a camera or a smoke test to find.

Fixture-level problem or line-level problem

The answer decides who fixes it.

Fixture level

  • Only one sink is slow.
  • Cleaning the stopper or trap helps.
  • You can try the fix yourself with basic tools.

Line level

  • Several drains are slow or gurgling.
  • The fix needs a cable, jetting or a camera.
  • Call for drain cleaning or sewer line cleaning.

Aerators, faucets and a false alarm

Sometimes the sink is fine and the faucet is the problem. A clogged aerator, the small screen at the tip of the spout, makes the water flow weakly, and it can look like the sink is filling slowly. Unscrew it, rinse out the grit and put it back.

If the faucet still runs poorly or drips, the cause may be in the valve. Our faucet and fixture repair page covers that side of the problem.

Coconut Creek note: which utility serves your home

A line problem sometimes comes down to which utility serves your street. The City of Coconut Creek utility serves residents north of Coconut Creek Parkway, and the City of Margate utility serves the area south of it. The city buys its water wholesale from Broward County.

The practical point is to know who to call if neighbors on your street have the same slow drains. If several households have the same symptom, a call to the utility serving your area helps rule out a shared problem. If it is only your house, the cause is almost always on your side of the property line.

Source: City of Coconut Creek water services page and City of Margate Utilities.

Habits that keep a sink flowing

A few small habits reduce slow drains.

  • Clean the stopper every few weeks.
  • Wipe greasy pans before washing them.
  • Run hot water for a minute after washing dishes.
  • Skip chemical cleaners, which can damage finishes and leave the film behind.
  • Call early if the drain gets slower each week.
