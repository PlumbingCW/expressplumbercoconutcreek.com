How to Change a Water Heater Element in Coconut Creek, FL

If an electric water heater runs out of hot water fast or only makes lukewarm water, a failed heating element is a common cause. Swapping one is a job many handy homeowners can do, as long as the power is off and proven dead first.

Check that the element is really the problem

This guide applies to electric tank heaters only. A gas heater has no elements, and a tankless unit has a different heating system. If you are not sure which type you have, the data plate on the side of the tank will say.

An electric tank usually has two elements, one near the top and one near the bottom. If the water is lukewarm, the lower element or a thermostat is a likely suspect. If you get a short burst of hot water and then cold, the upper element often fails. A tripped breaker or a tripped reset button on the heater can also cause the same symptoms.

Signs a heating element has failed

A bad element rarely fails all at once. These are the patterns we hear most.

Only lukewarm water

The thermostat is set high and the water still never gets properly hot.

Hot water runs out fast

Showers that used to last now turn cold halfway through.

The breaker trips

The breaker for the heater trips each time the heater tries to heat.

Humming or sizzling

A sizzling or humming sound from the tank can mean scale on the element.

Old tank, heavy sediment

Sediment around the lower element makes it burn out sooner.

Safety first: power off and proven dead

A water heater element runs on 240 volts, which can seriously injure or kill. Do these before you touch anything.

1

Switch off the breaker

Turn off the heater breaker at the panel and tape it so nobody switches it back on.

2

Remove the cover

Take off the access panel and push aside the insulation.

3

Test the wires

Check the terminals with a non-contact tester or a multimeter. If you cannot confirm the power is off, stop and call a licensed plumber.

How to replace an element, in order

Gather a multimeter, an element wrench, a replacement element that matches the voltage and wattage on the old one, and a bucket. Then work through these steps.

  1. 1

    Kill and verify power

    Switch off the breaker, remove the access panel and insulation, and test that the terminals read zero volts.

  2. 2

    Test the element

    Disconnect the wires, set the meter to resistance and touch the leads to the element terminals. A reading near zero or an open circuit points to a bad element.

  3. 3

    Close the supply and drain

    Shut the cold water valve, open a hot tap upstairs and drain the tank below the element level through the drain valve.

  4. 4

    Unscrew and swap

    Use the element wrench to remove the old element, fit the new one with its gasket and tighten it by hand and then wrench.

  5. 5

    Refill before power

    Open the cold valve with a hot tap open until water flows steadily, then restore power. Never power an element that is not underwater.

What the multimeter tells you

A resistance test is the simplest check you can do.

Why dry-firing destroys the new element

An element heats metal, and the tank water carries that heat away. If you power the element while the tank is still empty, it overheats in seconds and burns out. This is the mistake that turns a ten-minute repair into a second trip to the store.

The fix is simple. Keep a hot tap open as the tank fills and wait for a steady stream with no sputtering before the breaker goes back on.

Replace the element or replace the heater

A new element is cheap compared with a new tank, but only when the tank is sound.

Replace the element

  • Heater is young and shows no leaks at the base or seams.
  • Test points to one failed element and sediment is moderate.
  • The job fits in a single visit.

Replace the heater

  • Tank leaks, rusts at the seams or is past its normal service life.
  • Sediment is heavy and elements keep burning out.
  • See water heater replacement for what a new tank involves.

When to stop and call a plumber

Call if the wiring looks melted or scorched, if the element will not budge, or if you see water leaking from the tank body. A frozen element fitting can strip its threads and turn a small repair into a tank replacement. A licensed plumber has the right wrenches and the meter to confirm the diagnosis.

You can also hand the job over from the start. Our crews handle it under water heater repair, and the same visit covers the thermostat and the relief valve.

The point for homeowners is the pattern: storms in this region often interrupt power, and the return of power is a moment when water heaters deserve a check.

When power comes back, check that the breaker has not tripped, that the tank is full before you switch the heater on and that you see no leaks around the base. A heater that lost power for a long stretch can show element or thermostat trouble the first time it restarts. If the tank was submerged in flood water, do not switch it on, and call a licensed plumber and an electrician.
