Emergency drain cleaning in Coconut Creek, and what it involves

Drain cleaning is the removal of a blockage from a drain line so water flows freely again, using a cable machine, a hand auger or high-pressure water, and then confirming the line is clear. In Coconut Creek, FL, emergency drain cleaning is the right call when a toilet will not flush, a tub is filling with gray water or a kitchen sink has stopped completely.

Our line answers 24/7, and we send a technician the same day when the schedule allows. A stoppage at 11 p.m. or on a Sunday is still a stoppage, so nights and weekends get the same dispatch as weekday mornings.

When a clogged drain becomes an emergency

A single slow sink can wait for a daytime visit. Several drains failing together, or sewage showing up anywhere, cannot. Here is what to do while we are on the way.

1

Stop sending water down

Hold off on flushing, showers, laundry and the dishwasher. Every gallon you add has to go somewhere, and that somewhere is often your floor.

2

Check how many drains are affected

One slow fixture points to a local clog. A tub that gurgles when the toilet flushes, or two bathrooms backing up, points to the main line. Tell us which.

3

Keep people and pets out of wet areas

Backed-up water can carry bacteria. Keep children and pets away, and wear gloves and boots if you must walk through it.

4

Skip the chemical cleaners

Pouring a caustic cleaner into a standing blockage leaves it sitting in the pipe, and the technician then has to work around it. Plain water and a plunger are fine.

If sewage is coming up through floor drains or a shower, read our page on sewer line cleaning so you know what the main line stoppage visit involves.

Clogged drain symptoms, and where each usually starts

The pattern of what is slow tells us where to start. These are the symptoms homeowners describe most.

One sink drains slowly

The clog is usually in the trap or the first few feet of branch line from that fixture.

Toilet flushes weakly or rises and falls

Either the toilet trap is partly blocked or the line just beyond it is.

Gurgling from other drains

Air pushed out of one fixture is bubbling through another, a classic sign of a blockage deeper in the line.

Tub or shower fills with gray water

Hair, soap scum and a narrowed pipe are the usual culprits, often in the first stretch below the drain.

Water backs up in the lowest fixture

The shower or floor drain nearest the ground shows a main line problem first, because it sits lowest in the system.

A sour or sewage smell

A dry trap, a vent problem or a partial blockage can all cause it, and a camera shows which.

Kitchen sink clogs

The kitchen sink is the single most common drain we clear. Grease cools inside the pipe and coats the wall, food scraps catch on the coating, and over months the opening narrows to the width of a pencil.

If your sink has a garbage disposal, the clog is often just past the disposal outlet, which makes disposal repair and drain cleaning one visit. See our garbage disposal repair page if the unit is humming, jammed or leaking.

The fix depends on what we find. A hand auger reaches most trap and branch clogs. A grease buildup that keeps returning needs a cable with a cutting head, or hydro jetting to scour the pipe wall.

Drain snaking and cabling: how a clog is actually cleared

A drain snake, also called a drain cable or auger, is a coiled steel cable that is fed into the pipe and rotated by hand or by a machine. The tip either hooks the blockage and pulls it back, or breaks it apart so the water can flush it away.

Cable size matters. A small cable suits a bathroom sink, a mid-size machine suits a kitchen or tub line, and a larger drum machine with a cutting head is used on a main line. Using a cable that is too small can poke a hole through a clog and leave most of it behind.

After cabling, we run water for several minutes and watch how it moves. A line that drains fast for 30 seconds and then slows again is telling us the pipe itself is the problem, not just the clog.

Slow bathroom sinks and tubs

A bathroom sink that drains slowly but is not fully blocked is usually hair and soap scum wrapped around the pop-up stopper or sitting in the trap. Many people can lift the stopper and clean it in a few minutes.

If the stopper is clean and the sink is still slow, the trap or the branch behind it is the next suspect. A tub that fills around your ankles in the shower often has a hair mat just below the drain, which a small cable pulls out in one pass.

When two bathroom fixtures slow down together, stop treating them as separate problems. That pattern usually means a shared branch line is narrowing, and cabling one fixture will not solve it.

A leaking drain line is a different job

A drain that clears but leaves a damp cabinet, a stained ceiling below a bathroom or a musty crawl space may have a cracked or loose joint. Cleaning will not fix that. We confirm with a camera or a water test and then repair or replace the failed section.

What shapes drain problems in Coconut Creek homes

Local conditions change which clogs we see and what we recommend.

Older communities

City planning documents describe South Creek as more than 50 years old and Wynmoor as about 40 years old as of 2019. Drain lines that age can have rough interiors and shifted joints, so a stubborn clog may need a camera look before a second cleaning.

Wiped-down habits

Flushable wipes, paper towels and cooking grease cause many stoppages in any city. If your home shares a branch line with a kitchen, keep grease out of the sink because it hardens in the cooler stretches of pipe.

Heavy rain and storm season

The Atlantic hurricane season runs from June 1 to November 30. If drains slow down after a storm and nothing changed inside the house, the problem may be outside your property, and we help you work out which.

Who clears what

The City of Coconut Creek maintains local sewer infrastructure and Broward County treats the wastewater. A blockage inside your home or your own lateral is ours to clear, and one in the public main is for your utility.

What happens during a drain cleaning visit

  1. 1

    Locate the clog

    We ask which drains are slow, then test fixtures to see where the blockage sits.

  2. 2

    Choose the tool

    A hand auger, cable machine or jetter is picked to match the pipe and the clog.

  3. 3

    Clear and flush

    The blockage is broken up or pulled out, and the line is flushed with water.

  4. 4

    Test and explain

    We run the fixtures, tell you what we found and say whether a camera or jetting visit makes sense.

What affects the price of drain cleaning

Drain cleaning is quoted by the job, and the price depends on a few clear factors.

Which line is blocked

A sink trap is quicker than a main line that serves the whole house.

Access

A cleanout outside is easier to reach than a line that can only be entered through a toilet or a roof vent.

Tool needed

A hand auger, a cable machine and a jetter are different jobs with different time and equipment.

Cause of the clog

A food or hair clog is a one-time fix, while roots or a broken pipe need a larger plan.

Time of the call

After-hours and weekend dispatch is priced differently from a scheduled weekday visit, and you hear the number first.

Homeowner insurance usually does not pay to clear a clog caused by wear or buildup. Some policies add coverage for sewer backup damage, so check yours before you assume.

A quick checklist before you call

These details help us arrive with the right tools.

  • Which fixtures are slow or stopped, and which still work.
  • Whether water comes up in a shower or tub when you flush.
  • How long the problem has lasted and whether it came back after a past cleaning.
  • The location of your outside cleanout, if you know it.
  • Any chemical cleaner already poured in, since it changes how we handle the line.
