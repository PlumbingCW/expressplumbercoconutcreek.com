What we do

Coconut Creek Express Plumbing is a 24/7 plumbing line for Coconut Creek, FL. You call one number, describe the problem, and we dispatch licensed and insured plumbers to your address in Coconut Creek, Margate, Coral Springs, Parkland or Deerfield Beach.

We list the address 4400 W Sample Rd, Coconut Creek, FL 33073 as our base. Sample Road is the road that also leads to Tradewinds Park and Butterfly World, so most of our home-city calls are a short trip from there.

How a call works

You tell us what you see and where your main shutoff is. We tell you what to do while we dispatch, which is usually to close the main valve and stop using water.

The plumber diagnoses first, using meter tests, cameras or listening gear depending on the problem. You hear the cause, the repair options and the price before work starts, and you decide.

What we will not do

We do not quote prices over the phone for problems nobody has seen, because a leak under a slab and a leak under a sink are different jobs. We do not promise an arrival time, because traffic, storms and the job ahead of yours are real. We say what the first visit will tell you, and we put the price in front of you before work starts.

We also do not do roofing, pool shell repair or electrical work. If your problem turns out to be one of those, we tell you and point you to the right trade.

How to check any plumber

Whoever you hire, verify the license. Florida plumbing contractors are licensed by the Department of Business and Professional Regulation, and the state offers a Verify a Licensee search by name, license number or city. The City of Coconut Creek Building Department also advises hiring licensed, insured and reputable contractors.

For permit questions on your project, call the Coconut Creek Building Department at 954-973-6750. Permit rules vary by project and by city, so we do not guess.

Where we work

Our water facts come from city and county sources. The City of Coconut Creek utility serves homes north of Coconut Creek Parkway, the City of Margate utility serves homes south of it, and the city buys its water wholesale from Broward County. Our service areas page lists each town we dispatch to and the utilities there.

Services we dispatch for

The line handles problems that cannot wait and jobs you can schedule. Urgent calls usually involve leak detection, drain cleaning, burst pipe repair, sewer line repair or water heater repair, because water, sewage or a cold shower will not wait for Monday.

Planned work includes repiping, tankless water heater projects, plumbing installation and water softener and filtration work. Shops and offices can use the same line for commercial plumbing.

Why we say what we do not know

Plumbing answers depend on the property, and a good plumber admits that. Where a fact is not in front of us, such as a water hardness number for your street or a permit rule for your project, we send you to the agency that owns it instead of guessing.

That habit shows up on every page of this site. Local statements come from city, county and state pages, and the sources are named where it matters, so you can check them.

What a first visit covers

A first visit is about finding out what is wrong, not selling a job. The plumber looks at the fixture or line and asks what you noticed and when it started. Then comes the right test for the symptom: a pressure or meter test for a suspected leak, a camera for a drain that keeps backing up, a multimeter and a flush check for a water heater.

Then you get a plain explanation, the repair options in order from smallest to largest, and the price before any of it starts. If the smaller option will do, we say so. If it will not hold, we say that too, and why.

Some problems need a second visit for parts, permits or a larger crew. We tell you that at the start so the schedule does not surprise you.

Who the line is for

Homeowners and renters call us, and so do landlords, property managers and small businesses. A tenant with a leaking ceiling and a manager with a flooded stockroom are both plumbing calls, and both get the same first question about the main shutoff.

If you live in a community with an association, check whether the association owns part of the plumbing before you call. Shared stacks and common lines can change who pays and who is allowed to open a wall.

Reaching us

Calling is the fastest way. The contact page lists the number, the address and what to have ready, and the line answers around the clock.
