What a sewer camera inspection is and why people book one

A sewer camera inspection is a visit where a waterproof camera head is fed through your sewer line from a cleanout, sending live video to a screen. For Coconut Creek, FL homeowners, a sewer camera inspection answers a plain question: what is wrong inside that pipe, and where?

It is a planned visit, not an emergency one, though the results often explain an emergency you already had. Most calls come from three situations: a drain that clogs again and again, a home purchase, or a line that was cleaned and nobody knows why it stopped.

What the camera shows

The camera travels from the cleanout toward the street and shows the pipe wall as it goes. We look for a short list of problems, and each one points to a different fix.

Roots

Fine hairs or thick masses entering at a joint. Roots mean there is an opening, so cutting them is a short-term step, and sealing or lining the joint is the long-term one.

Bellies and sags

A low spot where the pipe has settled and water sits. Paper and waste collect there, and the clog keeps returning even after cleaning.

Cracks, breaks and offset joints

Hairline cracks, missing pieces and sections that no longer line up. The camera shows how many there are, which helps choose between a spot repair, lining or replacement, as covered on our sewer line repair page.

Buildup and debris

Grease, scale, a wipe wad or a foreign object stuck in the pipe. These often call for hydro jetting or a cable.

Buying a home? Check the sewer line first

A home inspection covers what can be seen, and the sewer lateral is buried. A camera pass before closing can show a root-filled joint or a collapsed section while you still have room to negotiate repairs.

We record the pass and review it with you, so you can share the footage with your agent. If the camera shows a problem, we can describe the repair options with a clear price before work starts. If the line is clean, you have one less unknown. Book the visit early, because inspection windows are short.

Repeat clogs and locating the line

If a plumber has cleared the same drain more than once, the next step is a camera and not a third cleaning. The footage shows whether the cause is a pipe that needs repair or a line that needs a jet.

The camera head also carries a transmitter that a locator can pick up from above ground. That lets us mark where the pipe runs and how deep the damage sits, which matters before anyone digs. Sunshine 811 asks you to call at least two full business days before any excavation, and we plan around that.

Local points that shape a camera visit

These facts affect what we look for and who you call about what.

Older neighborhoods

City planning documents describe South Creek as more than 50 years old and Wynmoor as about 40 years old as of 2019. If your home is in a community that age, ask for a full camera run, since older pipe can have joint problems.

Two sewer systems

The City of Coconut Creek maintains local sewer infrastructure and Broward County treats the wastewater. If the camera shows a problem out at the street, that part belongs to the utility, and we show you where our footage stops being your property.

Storm season

The Atlantic hurricane season runs from June 1 to November 30. Soil that has been soaked for days can shift a weak pipe, so a camera check after a wet stretch can catch a new sag early.

Permits

If the camera leads to a repair, ask the City of Coconut Creek Building Department at 954-973-6750 what paperwork applies. We do not guess at permit rules.

What happens during the visit

  1. 1

    Find the entry point

    We use an outside cleanout when there is one. Without it, the camera goes in through a toilet or vent.

  2. 2

    Run the camera

    The head travels the full length it can reach while we watch the screen and note anything unusual.

  3. 3

    Mark the location

    Any problem is located and marked above ground.

  4. 4

    Review together

    You see the footage, hear what it means and get options with a clear price before work starts.

What affects the cost of a sewer camera inspection

The inspection is quoted by the visit, and a few things move the number.

Access

A clean outside cleanout is quick, while a line with no cleanout takes longer to enter.

Line length

A short lateral and a long run through a large lot are not the same job.

Cleaning first

A very dirty line may need clearing before the camera can see, which is a separate step.

Locating and marking

Pinpointing a defect from above ground adds time.

Footage

Ask when you book what recording or report you need, such as for a buyer or an insurer.

Insurance does not usually pay for an inspection that simply checks a line. If the footage shows damage from a covered event, your insurer may want it as evidence, so keep it.
