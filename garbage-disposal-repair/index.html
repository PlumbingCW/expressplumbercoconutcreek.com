Emergency garbage disposal repair in Coconut Creek: start with the sound

A garbage disposal grinds food scraps under the kitchen sink so they wash down the drain, and when it fails the whole sink stops working. In Coconut Creek, FL, emergency garbage disposal repair usually means one of four things: a hum with no spinning, a leak under the cabinet, a full stop with no sound or water that will not drain.

We answer 24/7, so a jammed unit before a dinner party or a leak at midnight gets the same dispatch as a weekday call. Same-day visits are available when the schedule allows.

If the disposal is leaking or the sink is backing up

Dirty water under a sink soaks the cabinet floor quickly, and a backed-up basin can overflow. Do these steps while you wait for us.

1

Switch the disposal off at the wall

Flip the wall switch and, if you can reach it safely, unplug the unit from the outlet under the sink. Never put a hand inside the grinder, even when it is off.

2

Stop running water in the sink

Do not use the dishwasher either, since many dishwashers drain through the disposal and will push more water into a blocked sink.

3

Empty the cabinet and catch the drip

Pull out cleaners, set a bucket or tray under the leak and wipe up standing water so the base does not swell.

4

Keep power away from water

If the outlet under the sink is wet, leave the unit alone and switch off the circuit at the breaker only if the panel is dry.

If every drain in the house is slow, not just the kitchen sink, the problem is in the main line. Our sewer line cleaning team handles that.

Disposal problems, the way people describe them

Most calls start with a sound or a smell, not a part name. Here is what each one usually means.

It hums but does not spin

Something is jammed in the grinder, or the motor is stuck.

Nothing happens at all

The unit tripped its reset button, lost power or the motor has failed.

Water leaks under the sink

A loose drain connection, a failed seal or a cracked housing.

The sink drains slowly or backs up

Grease and food have clogged the drain line beyond the unit.

A bad smell won't go away

Food residue inside the grinder or a partial clog in the trap.

Loud grinding or rattling

A foreign object such as a bottle cap or utensil is stuck inside.

Repair or replace the disposal?

A disposal is often worth repairing when the issue is a jam, a loose connection or a tripped reset. Those fixes are quick and the unit keeps running afterward.

Replacement is the better choice when the housing leaks from the bottom, the motor has died or the unit is old and has jammed more than once. A leak through the casing cannot be sealed from the outside.

If you are buying a new unit, horsepower matters less than the build. A small household can get by with a half horsepower model, while a heavy cook may want a stronger motor and a sound-insulated case.

How a disposal replacement goes

A swap is a short visit when the sink flange is in good shape. Here is the order we work in.

  1. 1

    Power off and clear the cabinet

    We cut power to the unit, empty the cabinet and place a pan under the trap.

  2. 2

    Disconnect the old unit

    The drain pipe and dishwasher hose come off, and the mounting ring is twisted to release the disposal.

  3. 3

    Mount the new one

    We check the flange seal, seat the mounting ring and hang the new unit.

  4. 4

    Reconnect and test

    The drain and hose are reattached, power is restored and we run water to check every joint.

Habits that keep a disposal running

A few simple habits prevent most of the calls we get.

Run cold water

Run cold water before, during and for a short time after grinding so scraps move through the trap.

Skip the stringy and starchy foods

Celery, potato peels, pasta and rice can wrap or pack in the pipe.

Keep grease out

Pouring cooking grease down the drain lets it cool into a clog further along the line.

Use it often

Rust and food can build on a unit that sits unused for weeks. Run water and a little ice or citrus peel through it from time to time.

When the clog is in the drain and not the disposal

If water pools in the sink while the disposal runs, the grinder is probably fine and the line behind it is blocked. A double sink with one basin draining and the other filling points the same way.

We clear the trap and the drain beyond it, and if grease has built up in the line, our drain cleaning page explains how we remove it. A single sink that drains slowly can sometimes hide a deeper issue, so if the same clog returns, we may recommend a camera look.

Disposals and Coconut Creek kitchens

Many Coconut Creek homes and condos have a disposal under the kitchen sink, and the dishwasher often drains through it. When the disposal backs up, the dishwasher can leave dirty water in the bottom of the tub.

Condos and townhomes with shared drain stacks need special care, because a clog in a shared line can affect the unit below you. If water backs up in more than one unit, tell your association and call us. We also serve homes across Margate and Deerfield Beach with the same repair and replacement work.

What affects the cost of garbage disposal repair

A disposal visit can be a free-the-jam fix or a full replacement. These factors set the price.

Repair or replacement

Freeing a jam or resetting a seal is small. A new unit adds the disposal, mounting parts and removal of the old one.

Unit size and type

Continuous-feed and batch-feed models, and different horsepower ratings, are priced differently.

Electrical connection

A missing outlet or switch, or a hardwired connection, adds work before the new unit is hung.

Drain condition

A clogged trap or line adds clearing work to the visit.

After-hours visit

Emergency calls are priced differently from daytime repairs, and we tell you the price before work starts.

Insurance may cover sudden water damage under the sink but usually not the worn disposal itself. Take photos of any leak and ask your insurer what applies before repairs.
